Svashasan LabsMindGrove Technologies

Strategic partnership to ship Svashasan OS

We would like to propose a partnership in which Svashasan Labs remains the product and intellectual-property owner, while MindGrove Technologies becomes our applied software engineering and AI development partner, responsible for accelerating delivery. We are offering MindGrove a slightly larger share of the economics generated by the work it directly delivers, in exchange for taking meaningful engineering responsibility and helping us reach commercial pilots faster.

We propose starting with a 90-day joint engineering programme, rather than a broad strategic alliance, equity arrangement or transfer of core technology. Together we can prove that the partnership produces a working, measurable component of Svashasan OS, and then expand based on results.

1. Where the two companies fit together

We see MindGrove's strengths in applied AI, software development, custom model work, local model hosting and improving software delivery workflows. We believe those capabilities complement our existing simulated EV autonomy MVP, ROS 2-based integration work and longer-term ambition to build an operating-system layer for EVs.

We also want to be transparent about scope: we are not assuming that MindGrove has already delivered automotive-grade embedded software, production vehicle-control systems or functional-safety-certified software. For that reason, this partnership should initially focus on accelerating software delivery and validation, rather than on independently engineering a production-ready automotive OS.

The partnership structure

Svashasan Labs

Product owner · Automotive domain · Core technology

Owns Svashasan OS architecture, automotive integration requirements, core technology, product roadmap, safety boundaries, core IP and OEM relationships.

↕ Shared engineering interfaces, milestones, testing and commercial delivery

MindGrove Technologies

Joint outcome: A reproducible, testable Svashasan OS release candidate that can be demonstrated to prospective EV manufacturers and technology partners, followed by a controlled pilot.

Our guiding principle for the partnership: MindGrove owns delivery of its assigned workstream, while Svashasan retains stewardship of the product being delivered.

2. What we propose to build together

We are not proposing that MindGrove rewrite Svashasan OS or rebuild the autonomous-driving stack from scratch. That would create a large engineering dependency before the partnership has demonstrated value.

Instead, we propose a joint programme called the Svashasan OS Integration & Validation Platform.

This is an internal engineering layer that would help our combined team integrate, test, package and demonstrate the OS more reliably.

Workstream A: Build and release automation

Priority: Highest

MindGrove would set up reproducible builds, dependency management, CI pipelines, automated smoke tests, versioned release artifacts and engineering documentation.

Value to Svashasan: Less time spent manually setting up, integrating and debugging each release.

Workstream B: Simulation and regression testing

MindGrove would help automate the execution of agreed ROS 2 simulation scenarios, record logs, compare outputs against baselines and flag regressions.

Value to Svashasan: Every relevant code change can be tested against the same scenarios rather than relying on a successful manual demonstration.

Workstream C: Diagnostics and engineering intelligence

MindGrove would build a developer-facing diagnostic interface that organizes error logs, system events, performance measurements and simulation failures. An AI-assisted layer could help engineers search logs and summarize likely failure causes.

Value to Svashasan: Faster debugging and a clearer demonstration of system observability to future customers.

Workstream D: Integration-ready packaging

MindGrove would document and standardize module interfaces, dependency versions, configuration, installation procedures and release notes, and prepare the framework to expand from simulation into bench testing and hardware-in-the-loop testing when the relevant hardware is available.

Value to Svashasan: A more repeatable process for moving from simulation demonstrations toward real hardware integration.

These are proposed workstreams, not claims about MindGrove's existing automotive capabilities. We suggest confirming the assignment through a paid technical assessment of the relevant skills, so both sides are confident in the scope.

The first product milestone

Our proposed first joint deliverable is a reproducible Svashasan OS simulation release.

It would include:

  • A documented, reproducible development and build environment.
  • Automated execution of an agreed collection of simulation scenarios.
  • Versioned logs, test results and release artifacts.
  • A regression report identifying failed tests and performance changes.
  • An installation guide and a release acceptance checklist.
  • A demonstration showing the same software build passing the agreed test procedure consistently.

We would not market this milestone as an automotive-certified operating system. It is an engineering release candidate that improves the quality and credibility of the existing MVP.

5. Commercial model: a slightly larger share for MindGrove

We propose giving MindGrove an advantage in the joint programme's delivery economics, without equity or a permanent claim on all Svashasan OS revenue.

The 55/45 split below is our proposed starting point for discussion, and we welcome MindGrove's views on it.

Proposed economics

For the jointly delivered programme and separately contracted add-on modules

PartnerShareBasis
MindGrove Technologies55%Delivery and defined joint-module revenue share
Svashasan Labs45%Delivery and defined joint-module revenue share

These percentages apply only to the agreed partnership scope, not Svashasan's entire business.

How to implement that split

A. Joint pilot and integration services

For work sold and delivered jointly, allocate 55% of the agreed distributable service revenue to MindGrove and 45% to Svashasan, provided this matches the actual delivery commitments of each side.

We propose defining distributable revenue in the contract as cash actually collected, less agreed taxes, refunds and explicitly approved pass-through costs, with any overhead deductions agreed explicitly in advance, so both sides can see exactly how the split is calculated.

B. Separately priced, co-developed modules

For a module such as a diagnostic dashboard or simulation automation toolkit, we would apply a 55/45 net-receipts split in MindGrove's favour for the initial agreed commercial term, provided MindGrove has materially built and continues to support it.

The contract would set the term, maintenance obligations, minimum service levels and a review mechanism. A module that is no longer supported would not automatically continue generating the same revenue share indefinitely.

C. Core Svashasan OS licensing

Svashasan retains ownership of its core OS and is the lead commercial party for OS licensing. MindGrove would receive an agreed referral commission for qualifying customers it directly introduces and helps convert, plus fees or a revenue share for work it actually delivers.

For example, a separately negotiated first-year referral commission could be 10% of collected licence receipts from a customer originated by MindGrove. This is an illustrative starting point, not a market benchmark, and it would not stack automatically with other commissions or revenue shares.

D. Subsequent engineering engagements

If MindGrove helps win an OEM pilot or a paid integration project, it would earn more through the delivery share on that engagement. This encourages MindGrove to contribute beyond writing code and gives both of us a reason to commercialize the product together.

Example: a joint project

Suppose the two companies collect 100 monetary units in eligible service revenue after the explicitly agreed deductions.

AllocationAmount
MindGrove: 55%55 units
Svashasan: 45%45 units
Total100 units

This is revenue allocation, not profit. Each partner still covers the internal expenses it has agreed to bear.

The principle is to give MindGrove a better near-term economic position in exchange for meaningful delivery ownership. We propose delivering that advantage through the economics above, rather than through equity, core architecture ownership or an unrestricted share of future OS licensing.

6. How to structure the 90-day execution plan

Our plan assumes that the simulation MVP and relevant code repositories are made accessible to the joint engineering team. Hardware-dependent milestones would be added only when the necessary test hardware is available.

Phase 1: Technical and commercial discovery (Days 1–14)

MindGrove would review the existing repository, build setup, simulation workflow and test coverage. Both parties would agree on scope, interfaces, delivery estimates, security controls and acceptance tests.

Gate: Approved technical assessment, deliverables, baseline performance and statement of work.

Phase 2: Engineering infrastructure (Days 15–30)

We would implement reproducible builds, dependency control, automated smoke tests, logging conventions and a defined release workflow.

Gate: The agreed software can be built and tested using documented instructions in the designated environment.

Phase 3: Automated simulation and diagnostics (Days 31–60)

We would automate the agreed scenario suite, generate regression reports, package artifacts and implement the initial diagnostic interface.

Gate: The team can detect an intentionally introduced test failure and produce an understandable report without relying on a manually assembled test run.

Phase 4: Release candidate and external demonstration (Days 61–90)

We would freeze a defined software version, resolve critical test failures, document remaining limitations and prepare a customer-facing technical demonstration.

Gate: Svashasan accepts the release against agreed criteria and approves the demonstration package.

Suggested acceptance criteria

We would agree on numeric targets in the first two weeks after measuring the current baseline. Our initial proposal is:

MetricProposed target
Reproducible build procedureSuccessfully executed in the agreed clean environment
Agreed critical smoke tests100% passing before release acceptance
Selected recurring simulation scenarios automatedAt least 80%
Build and test outputGenerated and versioned for every release-candidate run
Critical defectsNone unresolved at acceptance
DocumentationComplete build, test, installation and rollback instructions

These are proposed programme targets, not claims about current performance or certification. The 80% test-automation target applies only to the selected scenarios in scope.

7. Automotive safety and cybersecurity boundaries

This is the area where we intend to be particularly disciplined, and we want to set expectations openly.

ISO 26262 addresses functional safety in road vehicles, including safety-management processes and software development. ISO/SAE 21434 addresses automotive cybersecurity engineering across the vehicle electronics lifecycle. UNECE Regulations No. 155 and No. 156 address vehicle cybersecurity and software-update management, respectively. Their applicability and evidence requirements depend on the target vehicle, market and approval pathway.

Accordingly:

  • MindGrove would not be presented as a functional-safety or automotive-cybersecurity specialist unless its credentials and capabilities have been verified.
  • No unvalidated AI model or LLM will be placed in the real-time braking, steering or other safety-critical control path.
  • We will establish code review, version control, test traceability, defect management and change approval before accepting deliverables.
  • Vehicle data and proprietary source code stay within explicitly approved access and data-handling boundaries.
  • An appropriately qualified automotive engineering and safety team will assess the path from simulation to hardware and eventually vehicle deployment.

MindGrove can help generate the engineering evidence and automate test procedures. A working pipeline alone does not make the overall OS safe or compliant, and we will plan for that separately.

8. How both sides win commercially

What MindGrove gains

A meaningful software delivery engagement, favourable economics on agreed joint projects, the opportunity to develop reusable engineering capabilities and a demonstrable reference implementation. With Svashasan's permission, MindGrove could use anonymized or approved case-study results to pursue other software engineering clients.

What Svashasan gains

Faster engineering iteration, better test repeatability, lower manual integration burden, a more credible release process and a stronger demonstration for OEMs and potential technology partners. Svashasan retains its core IP and the customer relationship.

Joint commercial opportunity

Once the engineering package is stable, our companies can offer a defined integration and validation engagement to an EV OEM, Tier 1 supplier or other suitable mobility partner. The first external engagement should be a paid proof of concept, with scope and success criteria agreed in advance.

Our joint commercial story will focus on what we can actually prove: reproducible testing, faster release cycles, measurable defect detection and easier integration. We will not claim a particular reduction in development cost or integration time until a baseline and comparison substantiate it.

9. Intellectual property and contract structure

The agreement would distinguish between three categories of technology.

CategoryProposed treatment
Existing Svashasan IPRemains exclusively with Svashasan
Existing MindGrove IP and general-purpose toolsRemains with MindGrove, subject to the agreed licence terms for use within Svashasan OS
New Svashasan-specific modules and modificationsAssigned to Svashasan, subject to legal review and the final agreement; MindGrove receives the negotiated commercial share

The contract would also explicitly cover source-code access, third-party and open-source licences, confidentiality, data use, subcontractors, acceptance criteria, warranties, support, termination and transfer of unfinished work.

To protect both parties' IP, we propose that Svashasan confidential code and proprietary vehicle data are not used to train or improve external models without explicit written approval, and that accepted deliverables are held in repositories controlled by Svashasan, with sufficient documentation for another engineering team to maintain them.

We propose a master services agreement plus a narrowly defined statement of work for the first 90 days. The arrangement would be non-exclusive, with clearly scoped rights in place of automatic equity, permanent OS royalties or open-ended platform rights.

Proposed immediate next steps

  1. MindGrove nominates the engineers who would work on Svashasan OS and shares relevant examples of production software they have built.
  2. Svashasan shares a carefully scoped technical brief, and MindGrove returns a delivery proposal for the integration and validation platform.
  3. Both sides finalize the 55/45 economics once the scope, staffing and acceptance criteria are clear.

To keep the partnership focused and low-risk for both sides, we propose starting without upfront equity or an open-ended development commitment, and expanding as results are delivered.

Final recommendation

Svashasan Labs offers MindGrove Technologies a slightly superior share of the engineering and co-developed-module economics and real ownership of delivery, while Svashasan stays in control of the core OS, automotive safety decisions, customer relationship and long-term licensing business.

We believe the partnership earns the right to expand by shipping a working release candidate in 90 days, not by signing a broad collaboration announcement. We would be glad to start with MindGrove.
